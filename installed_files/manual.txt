--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Touch controls
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Gestures:
Swipe - MOVE one step in the swiped direction

Tap - CONFIRM, or mark a list entry

Hold + drag - LOOK around (pan the map)

Two-finger tap - Show or hide the on-screen KEYBOARD

Back button - CANCEL, or close the current screen (same as the [ x ] control in the top right corner)

Moving can also be done with an on-screen movement pad instead of swiping (see the "Movement" setting). The pad is placed in the bottom corner opposite the stats panel - hold it to move or resize it, and tap elsewhere to put it down.

Many things in the game can be interacted with by moving into them (i.e. by "bumping" them), for example:

- Move into monsters to melee attack them

- Move into closed doors, chests, cabinets, etc to attempt to open them

- Move into opened chests, cabinets, etc to loot them

- Move into stuck doors to attempt to bash them open

- Move into fountains to drink from them

While looking (hold + drag), the location under the marker is described, and buttons appear for anything that can be done there - describing a monster in detail, opening, closing or jamming a door, picking up or unloading an item, and so on. Looking is free - no game time passes.

The button bar at the bottom of the screen holds the most common commands. The leftmost button opens the game menu, which also contains the character description, message history, settings, and this manual. Which buttons are shown, and in which order, is configured in the game menu under "Actions".

The target and throw buttons toggle aiming: drag to aim, then loose with the [ fire ] or [ throw ] button. Tapping the bar button again - or swiping to move - cancels the aiming.

When the game asks a question, the answers are shown as buttons above the button bar.

Keyboard commands:

The on-screen keyboard (or a connected hardware keyboard) reaches every command, including a few without buttons:

b - Use MEDICAL BAG

c - CLOSE DOOR, or jam closed door with an iron spike

C - CHARACTER information

f - AIM/FIRE weapon

g - PICK UP item

h - MESSAGE history

i - INVENTORY (wield, equip, remove, consume, or activate items)

k - KICK or strike objects, or DESTROY CORPSES

l - Toggle LANTERN

m - Show MAP (or close map)

n - Make NOISE

p - DISARM trap

r - RELOAD

s - WAIT five turns, or until something happens

t - THROW item, throw lit EXPLOSIVE

u - UNLOAD firearm on the ground, or pick up item

v - LOOK, VIEW descriptions of things on the map

x - CAST spell

z - SWAP to readied weapon

. (5) - WAIT one turn

A hardware keyboard can also move with the numpad or arrow keys (hold shift or ctrl with left/right to move diagonally).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The game screen
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Beside the map (on the left side by default - swipe the panel toward the nearest screen edge to move it to the other side), information is listed about your character and your carried equipment. The following is an example of what these lines can look like:

Level 1 (60%) - Character level, and progress towards next level. This progress is called experience, or "XP".

Depth 2 - Current floor (or "dungeon level").

Health 9/16 - Current and maximum Hit Points. See "Health and wounds" for more info.

Spirit 8/8 - Current and maximum Spirit Points. See "Spells and spirit" for more info.

Shock 45% - Your level of shock or stress. See "Insanity" for more info.

Insanity 12% - Your level of insanity. See "Insanity" for more info.

Wpn (+1) 8.0 +0% - Your currently wielded weapon. "(+1)" means that this weapon does one extra point of damage compared to normal weapons of the same type. "8.0" is your average damage with this weapon (including skills affecting the damage). "+0%" is this weapon's hit chance modifier (the total hit chance is affected by several other factors, such as your character's skill and the creature attacked).

Alt 8.5 +0% 4/7 - Your prepared (alternative) weapon. The weapon in this example is a firearm, "4/7" is the weapon's loaded and maximum ammunition.

Lantern 100 - Remaining number of turns that your Electric Lantern can be used for.

Med. Suppl. 24 - Remaining Medical Bag Supplies. See "Health and wounds" for more info.

Armor 3 - Armor Points. Each point reduces physical damage taken by 1.

Weight 40% - Encumbrance from carrying items. From 100% you move at half speed, and from 125% you cannot move at all.

Turn 123 - Current standard turn number. Note that the player can sometimes act more or less often than these turns are ticking (e.g. if they have the "hasted" or "slowed" effects applied).

Blind - You are currently blind.

Diseased - You are currently diseased.

The properties at the bottom can be temporary properties (e.g. from spells), PERMANENT intrinsic properties (printed in capital letters, e.g. "CURSED"), or properties granted by wearing or wielding items.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Inventory
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The inventory can be accessed in two ways:

(1) The inventory button opens a full view of your inventory

Here, you can see all of your inventory slots (see below), and a complete list of your carried items. Selecting an inventory slot either puts away an existing item from the slot, or opens a list of items that you can equip in the slot. Selecting a carried item shows its description, with buttons for what can be done with it - equip, consume, activate, drop, and so on.

Items are equipped in different inventory slots:

Wielded - your currently used melee weapon or firearm

Prepared - the weapon to switch to when swapping weapons

Body - item worn on your body (e.g. an armor or a jacket)

Head - item worn on your head (e.g. a gas mask)

(2) The throw button opens a list to select an item to throw

Selecting an item in this menu will show an aiming marker on the map, to select where to throw the chosen item.

Note that explosives are not used like throwing weapons. Instead they are used by first igniting or priming the explosive, this is done by "activating" it from the inventory - now you can see your character colored yellow as a warning that you are holding an ignited explosive. Open the throw list and aim somewhere (in some cases the game will mark the area around your aim position to indicate the explosion size - be careful though, there may be unseen objects blocking the throw path), then loose with the [ throw ] button.

Each item's weight, and the percentage of your total carried weight it takes, is shown in its description. So if you are overburdened, an item with a high percentage value might be a good candidate to drop.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Health and wounds
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The state of your physical health is described in two ways:

(1) Hit Points (HP)

This represents minor injuries such as bruises, sprains, or light bleeding - and also general combat morale, fatigue, stance, etc. If your Hit Points reach 0, it means you received a killing blow, or the circumstances are such that you can no longer fight back (you are fallen, pinned or completely exhausted). Hit Points usually regenerate automatically over time.

(2) Wounds

This represents more serious long-term damage. Wounds are received when you take a high amount of damage in one hit. Each Wound reduces your fighting abilities, maximum HP, and HP regeneration rate. Five concurrent wounds results in death.

There is an item called a "Medical Bag", which has a certain number of "supplies". When activated, it can be used to either sanitize an infection, treat a WOUND, or do a quick patch-up that restores some Hit Points. When bitten or clawed by certain monsters, you can become Infected; This should be treated before the infection turns into a disease! Treating infections or doing a quick patch-up only require a few supplies, while treating wounds requires more.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Spells and spirit
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In addition to your physical and mental health, you have an "essence" - or Spirit - to preserve. If you lose all your Spirit, you are dead.

Your Spirit Points are shown like this: "Spirit 8/8" (current and maximum).

You can learn spells by casting them from manuscripts. Casting spells "naturally" (without manuscripts) will drain some of your spirit.

To cast memorized spells, use the cast button to open a menu for selecting which spell to cast. Each spell's description shows its Spirit cost (the cost is a range of values, and the spirit drained is a random value in this range), and your skill level for the spell (Occultist characters can learn to cast spells at higher levels by picking certain traits).

When casting a spell, you will get a warning if the spell could potentially drain all your remaining Spirit. You can then choose to attempt casting or abort. Casting the spell in this situation is a gamble with death.

There are also other ways you can lose Spirit, for example some monsters can drain your Spirit with their attacks.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Insanity
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"A few steps more, and our breaths were literally snatched from us by what we saw; so literally that Thornton, the psychic investigator, actually fainted in the arms of the dazed man who stood behind him. Norrys, his plump face utterly white and flabby, simply cried out inarticulately; whilst I think that what I did was to gasp or hiss, and cover my eyes. The man behind me - the only one of the party older than I - croaked the hackneyed "My God!" in the most cracked voice I ever heard. Of seven cultivated men, only Sir William Brinton retained his composure; a thing the more to his credit because he led the party and must have seen the sight first."-H.P. Lovecraft, "Rats in the Walls"

Certain events in this game affects the sanity and composure of your character. One of the main threats to your mental health are the various horrifying creatures that inhabit the game world. When inside your view, some creatures may cause a vague unease, while others are very disturbing to behold. Your mind also takes a hit when you use occult powers, carry weird artifacts, or stand in darkness, etc. Finally, there is a slow perpetual taxing of your mind from exploring and dwelling in this haunted dungeon.

Your mental health is represented by two percentage values:

- "Shock" is your level of stress and paranoia in the current situation

- "Insanity" is long term, permanent madness

When various disturbing events occur, the shock value rises. When shock hits 100%, the following happens:

- You react somehow (scream, laugh, faint, babble, gain a phobia...)

- The shock value is restored

- Insanity rises

Each time you travel to the next dungeon level, your shock is restored (you have escaped the horrors above, and are bestowed with a sense of progress). Also, standing in a lit area reduces your shock a bit (most characters start with an Electric Lantern, which can be useful to prevent shock from rising too high).

If your Insanity reaches 100%, you are hopelessly lost, and the game is over.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sneaking
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Monsters unaware of your presence have a blue background on their map icon. This is their default mode. While in this state they may roam the map, but they will not actively search for you or purposefully attack you. If you attack an unaware opponent in melee, the attack does +50% extra damage, and has a very high chance to hit.

For each turn you spend in a monsters visual area (for monsters with sight - some monsters are naturally blind), there is a chance that it will either completely detect you, or become "suspicious". While suspicious, the monster has an increased chance of detecting you (watch out for messages about monsters looking "wary" or "disturbed").

Some monsters have the ability to sneak as well. You automatically attempt to detect them while they are in your visual area.

The chances of one creature detecting another are affected by:

- Search skill

- The hidden actor's sneak skill

- Light and darkness

- Distance

When you stay out of an aware monster's vision for a certain number of turns (and nothing else alerts it), it will "forget" about you. The number of turns this takes depends on the monster; A simple animal may only care for a couple of turns after it loses sight, while a human will remain aware longer.

Monsters also react to noise, and may disturb nearby monsters when becoming aware, alerting them to your presence. Medium and heavy melee weapons will make noise on attacks, so a light weapon might be more ideal for a stealthy combat approach. Some sounds are louder than others, for example firing a gun or kicking down a door is likely to attract some attention.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scores and memorials
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Winning is not necessarily the sole purpose of Infra Arcana - there is also challenge to be found in exploring further, gaining more abilities, discovering more items, or encountering stranger and more powerful monsters than in previous attempts.

After each game is finished, information such as player name and dungeon level reached is stored as a "memorial", and a high score entry is added (unless you manually quit the game from the menu). The high score list can be viewed by selecting the main menu option "Graveyard". Selecting a high score entry on this screen will show the corresponding memorial.

In addition to this, the top high score entries show up as gravestones on the first level of the game (in the forest). Bumping a gravestone shows a short summary of the high score entry.

Score values are calculated based on your experience points (including points gained after the maximum character level is reached), which is then multiplied by several factors.

The factors contributing to a higher score value are:

- Experience points gained

- Dungeon level reached

- Fewer turns spent

- Lower insanity

Also, when winning the game, some large bonus factors are added. Every winning game is guaranteed to have a better score than any non-winning game.
